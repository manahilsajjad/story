## Welcome to town ##
It was a cold and stormy night when I moved to the small town. I knew immediately that something was wrong with the place. Not just strange wrong.
There were no children in the streets, no chalk drawings on the pavement—only silence, and the red balloons. They were as red as blood. They hung everywhere: on the lampposts, near the storm drains. I asked my father why there were red balloons. Without looking at me, he said quickly, "It’s clearly just a festive tradition." The way he said it felt wrong; I didn't know why, but there was something off about his voice.
## The Warning ##
Later that evening, I looked out the window. I saw the old lady in the house across the street saying something. At first, I didn't understand, but then I did. She said, "If you see a red balloon glowing, run and never look back." I didn't know why, but it sent shivers down my spine. I started to close the curtain. Then, I noticed something strange on her neck—black lines that looked like stitches. I screamed when I realized her head had been stitched together. But she just smiled and whispered, "Remember! What I said."
## The Glowing Balloon ##
Afterward, I tried to sleep but I couldn't. I kept tossing and turning in bed. Then, I saw it: a red balloon floating outside my window—it was glowing. I sat up. The balloon slowly drifted closer. *Thud!* It hit the windowpane. *Thud! Thud! Thud!* Then, it stopped. Behind the balloon stood a boy. His face was pale. And his skin looked strange—like a mask. Then, a piece of the skin slid off. I gasped; it wasn’t a mask. It was just raw red flesh and dark eyes. The boy stared straight at me, then pointed toward the basement and vanished.
## Something in the Basement ##
My parents woke up because of the noise. My father went down to the basement, taking a baseball bat with him. Then it became quiet—so quiet that I could hear nothing but my own heart....pounding. The air felt thick with humidity. A cold drop of sweat trickled down my back.
A strange, scraping sound came from the basement. Then, a terrible scream rang out—it was my father. My mother’s face turned pale, and she shouted, "Lock the room!" As I locked it, my mother’s hands began to tremble. We both stood perfectly still. There was a heavy, creaking footstep. Something was drawing closer. I could taste a metallic tang in my mouth. The doorknob slowly turned. I was taken aback—I had locked the door, after all. But I had no time to wonder how it had been opened, because the person who stepped inside looked just like my father. Yet, something was wrong. His smile was far too wide. His eyes didn't blink. His skin looked stretched, as if it didn't quite fit his face. And around his neck ran a line of black stitches. The skinless boy had donned my father’s skin like a jacket. He walked calmly and quietly into the room, while my mother and I retreated backward.
## The Living Toys ##
I closed my eyes, praying that this was just a dream. But when I looked around, the toys began to move. The teddy bears slowly turned their heads; their eyes stared straight at me. A toy car began to roll across the floor on its own, and the dolls on the shelf blinked. All the other toys crawled down from the shelves. Small hands dragged themselves across the floor.
Suddenly, the old woman appeared in the corner. Her skin split open. Beneath it, there was wood. Rusty nails protruded from her neck. She was a doll. Outside the window stood several people. They were all staring in. They were all dolls.
## The Boy Behind the Balloon ##
Then the skinless boy stepped toward me and touched my shoulder. A red balloon hovered above his head. It glowed so intensely that the entire room was bathed in red light. “All these people belong to the balloon now,” he whispered. I closed my eyes.
## The Note ##
When I opened my eyes again, everything was gone. The basement was empty. The toys stood motionless. Dad was cooking in the kitchen. But when I looked at the kitchen counter, there was a note that read: *I’ll be back, Ellias.* I wondered how the skinless boy knew my name—I had never told him. But I just tried to forget it all, and I succeeded. After that, everything went back to normal; I went to school, ate my meals, and played with my friends.
But on some nights, I still think I can hear a faint *THUD* outside the window.
